What does the ring encode?

The ring is already a gauge — it has a start, a sweep and an end, and people read that as data whether or not we mean it. Four options below that don't depend on being in a training season, plus one that stops it being a gauge at all.

The ring as a gauge — same character, five fill levels

Option A — how you compare to your own best (recommended)

Your current rolling fitness against the highest it has ever been. Full ring means you are in the best shape of your life; a gap means there is ground to make back. This is the app's entire thesis rendered on the character — the mascot literally wears the answer to "am I getting better". It can fall, but a training partner who keeps score should show it when you slip.

Option B — the next distance milestone

Lifetime kilometres toward the next round number. Only ever climbs, never resets, works from your first run, and needs no training context at all. The safest option — and the dullest, since it can sit visually unchanged for weeks.

Option C — closing on a personal record

How near you are to your PR at whichever distance you run most. Directly on-theme — you versus your past self — and it has a real payoff moment when it completes. Downside: it is only meaningful if you are actually chasing a time, and it sits near-full forever once you set a PR you cannot beat.

Option D — not a gauge at all

Make it a complete ring rather than a partial arc, and let it carry mood through colour and weight instead of fill. Because it is closed, nobody reads it as a progress meter — which removes the problem rather than solving it. The character still ties to the mark, and the ring becomes expressive rather than informational.

Worth knowing: D is the only option with no data dependency, so the mascot renders identically for a brand-new user with zero runs. Every other option has an empty state where the ring is nearly bare — which is the exact moment a new user forms their first impression.
